---
slug: heel-drop
title: Heel drops
shortCue: Up onto your toes, then drop sharply onto your heels, hands on the chair back
image: ../../../assets/exercises/c4-heel-drop.png
imageAlt: A woman stands on her toes next to a chair, ready to drop sharply onto her heels
category: power
muscles: [gastrocnemius, soleus]
equipment: [chair]
steps:
  base:
    description: Rise onto your toes next to a support, drop onto your heels with a moderate impact
  working:
    description: Rise onto your toes, drop sharply onto your heels with a noticeable impact
  advanced:
    description: Same movement, fingertips only lightly touching the chair back
technique:
  - Hold the chair back lightly
  - Rise onto your toes, then drop your heels sharply to the floor
  - The impact should be noticeable but not painful
mistakes:
  - From week 3 on, the drop must come with a noticeable heel impact; without it the bone gets no load
  - The knees bend on the drop instead of the legs staying straight
  - Landing on the toes or the side of the foot
stopSigns:
  - Sharp pain in the foot or shin after the impact
  - Knee pain when landing on the heels
  - Back pain after a heel drop
osteoporosisNote: With diagnosed osteoporosis the exercise stays unchanged; do not remove the heel impact. After a vertebral compression fracture, ask your doctor first
---

Gives your bones the impact load they need to build density. This is a separate goal from plain muscle strength.
